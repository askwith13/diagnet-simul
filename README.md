# DiagNet — Diagnostic Network Intelligence for TB Care

AI-powered simulation platform for India's National TB Elimination Programme (NTEP).
Demonstrates multi-hub network intelligence across 5 districts, 59 facilities, and 8 scenarios.

**Hackathon submission — June 9-10, 2026 · PATH South Asia Digital Health**

---

## What DiagNet Is — and Is Not

**DiagNet is not a dashboard. It is not a standalone application.**

DiagNet is an AI intelligence layer that sits on top of existing health data infrastructure — Nikshay, LIMS, facility inventory systems — and reasons continuously about diagnostic network health. Its only interaction with front-end users is through **notifications**: actionable alerts delivered via SMS, WhatsApp, email, Nikshay pop-ups, and LIMS workflow triggers. A lab technician at a district hospital centre does not open DiagNet. They receive a WhatsApp message that says: *"Redirect MC2-A and CHC2-A referrals to TL2-A now. Cascade dropout risk at 41%."* That message is DiagNet.

The visualisation you see in this simulation — the network maps, the bottleneck heatmaps, the SHAP charts — exists to **show the inner workings of the AI**, not to serve as a user interface. It makes the model's reasoning transparent to programme managers, district officers, and technical evaluators. In production, the same reasoning happens silently in the background. The only output a field user ever sees is the notification.

### How DiagNet fits into the ecosystem

```
Nikshay / LIMS / Facility inventory
         ↓  (data feeds)
   DiagNet AI engine
  ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │  Bayesian stockout forecasting                        │
  │  Cascade dropout risk modelling                      │
  │  Network flow optimisation (Dijkstra rerouting)      │
  │  SHAP-explained anomaly detection                    │
  └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
         ↓  (workflow-triggered alerts)
  SMS · WhatsApp · Email · Nikshay pop-up · LIMS alert
         ↓  (reaches the right person)
  Lab supervisor · DTO · CHW coordinator · Programme manager
```

No new login. No new app to learn. Alerts arrive in the channels field staff already use, triggered by real workflow events (specimen registration, test completion, stock adjustment), not by manual polling.

## The 8 Scenarios

Each scenario is a pre-computed simulation of a realistic failure mode or intervention pathway across the 5-district network. Ticks represent operational days. All alerts are generated by the AI engine and delivered as the notifications described above.

### SC-01 · Network Bottleneck — NET-A Chandrapur (25 days, Reactive)

A single district hospital centre (DHC-A) becomes the dominant referral hub for Chandrapur. Queue pressure builds as upstream microscopy centres (MC2-A, CHC2-A) funnel all presumptive TB cases to DHC-A without load balancing. TAT doubles within 4 days, triggering a critical alert to the lab supervisor. By day 7, the AI identifies TL2-A as an underutilised alternative and recommends redirecting two feeder facilities. Even after the reroute eases hub pressure, patients travelling to TL2-A face longer journeys — cascade dropout risk continues rising, prompting CHW activation at day 10. Day 14 marks a district officer advisory: the bottleneck needs permanent staffing relief, not just rerouting.

**Key concepts demonstrated:** Hub saturation detection · Dijkstra-optimal rerouting · Cascade dropout as a lagging indicator that outlasts queue relief.

---

### SC-02 · Gradual Stockout — NET-B Balaghat (25 days, Reactive)

Truenat chips at Taluka Hospital Balaghat (TH-B) deplete steadily over a campaign month as test volume rises. The AI detects the decline early (day 5) and flags it as a low-threshold warning — too early for emergency action, early enough to place a routine order with 3–5 days lead time. By day 12 the window has closed: chips are critically low and emergency procurement is the only option. Day 16 triggers a DTO advisory with a probability-of-stockout score. Day 20 is the worst case: testing suspends and all queued patients must overflow to TL1-B and TL2-B, which the AI orchestrates automatically.

**Key concepts demonstrated:** Early-warning vs. emergency procurement windows · Bayesian stockout probability as a decision trigger · The cost of acting on day 5 vs. day 20.

---

### SC-03 · Predicted Stockout (Bayesian) — NET-C Yavatmal (25 days, Preventive)

The same stockout trajectory as SC-02, but intercepted before it becomes reactive. The Bayesian model at day 6 assigns a 40%+ probability of stockout within 14 days based on current consumption rate and stock level — well before any visible crisis. The AI recommends a preventive reroute of MC3-C and MC4-C to TL2-C for 7 days, conserving DHC-C cartridges by reducing the number of patients reaching the hub. By day 18, the stockout probability has fallen below 15% and normal routing resumes. No procurement emergency required.

**Key concepts demonstrated:** Bayesian early intervention vs. reactive response · Rerouting as a consumable conservation strategy · The value of probabilistic forecasting over threshold-based alerting.

---

### SC-04 · Cascade Failure + Inter-Network — NET-D Gadchiroli → NET-A (30 days, Reactive)

The most complex single-network failure: DHC-D (Gadchiroli district hub) simultaneously faces queue surge, chip depletion, and machine failure. By day 8, inter-network overflow is activated — complex cases from Gadchiroli are rerouted to DHC-A (Chandrapur), a cross-district transfer that NTEP programme managers must explicitly authorise. The downstream effect is DHC-A absorbing foreign patient load on top of its own, raising cascade risk across both networks. Day 18 triggers a system-wide DTO briefing request — this is no longer a facility-level problem.

**Key concepts demonstrated:** Multi-failure compounding · Inter-network overflow as a last-resort escalation · Cross-district coordination as a programme management, not just operational, event.

---

### SC-05 · Concurrent Multi-Failure — NET-E Amravati (30 days, Reactive)

DHC-E (Amravati) hits two critical thresholds simultaneously: cartridge stock falls below the safe level at the same time patient queue exceeds critical capacity. Because the failures are concurrent rather than sequential, the usual triage logic (fix the worst first) doesn't apply — both must be addressed in parallel. The AI identifies CHC3-E and CHC4-E as the optimal facilities to reroute (Dijkstra shortest-path to TL2-E), buying TAT relief while emergency procurement is arranged. Day 22 shows partial recovery: queue is falling but cartridges remain critical, requiring continued reroute until resupply confirms.

**Key concepts demonstrated:** Concurrent failure vs. sequential failure response · Parallel intervention (reroute + procurement simultaneously) · Extended reroute as a bridge until physical resupply arrives.

---

### SC-06 · Machine Downtime — NET-A Chandrapur (20 days, Reactive)

A GeneXpert module fails at DHC-A with only 2 ticks of warning. TAT begins climbing immediately as fewer modules share the same test volume. Day 6 triggers a Bayesian anomaly alert: the test-ordering pattern deviates from the model baseline in a way that suggests a secondary issue beyond the known module failure — possibly specimen diversion, underreporting, or a second failing module. The AI flags this for investigation rather than assuming the machine failure explains everything. Day 12 escalates to the DTO: TAT has crossed the critical threshold and queued patients must be redistributed to satellite labs while the engineer works.

**Key concepts demonstrated:** Anomaly detection on top of known events (the AI doesn't turn off when a failure is already known) · Engineer dispatch as a DTO-level, not lab-level, decision · TAT as the early leading indicator of machine health.

---

### SC-07 · Campaign Month Surge — NET-B Balaghat (25 days, Predictive)

A TB active case-finding campaign drives a surge in presumptive case registrations at TH-B. Daily chip consumption rises 40% above baseline. The AI detects the surge pattern at day 4 and calculates stockout probability against the current trajectory — even though stock levels look superficially adequate, the elevated burn rate makes the 14-day runway unacceptable. Day 8 triggers a proactive redistribution recommendation: transfer chips from TL2-B (underutilised) to TH-B before stock falls below the warning threshold. The redistribution succeeds: stockout probability falls, campaign month ends without testing disruption.

**Key concepts demonstrated:** Campaign-aware demand forecasting · Proactive redistribution before visible shortage · The contrast between stock-level alerting (lagging) and consumption-rate alerting (leading).

---

### SC-08 · Full Network Recovery — All 5 Networks (35 days, Recovery)

Starts from a post-crisis state: all five district hubs are critical simultaneously, with the assumption that emergency interventions (procurement, machine repair, CHW deployment) have already been mobilised but not yet arrived. This scenario demonstrates the AI's role during the recovery arc — not just detecting problems but tracking the pace of resolution. Cartridge and chip procurement replenish at defined rates per network. Queue clears as reroutes remain active. The AI tracks cascade risk at each hub and explicitly instructs coordinators not to stand down CHW teams until risk falls below 25%. Full green status is reached across all five networks by day 25, with a final DTO handoff advisory at day 35.

**Key concepts demonstrated:** Multi-network simultaneous recovery coordination · Staged step-down protocols (don't reduce CHW before risk threshold) · The AI as a recovery monitor, not just a crisis detector.

## Quick Start (Local)

```bash
# 1. Install dependencies
uv add streamlit plotly networkx scipy pandas pyarrow pydantic pyyaml kaleido openpyxl
uv add --dev pytest pytest-cov ruff

# 2. Generate pre-computed scenario data (run once; re-run if YAMLs change)
uv run python scripts/generate_scenarios.py --all --validate

# 3. Launch the app
uv run streamlit run app.py
```

The app opens at http://localhost:8501.

## Regenerating Scenario Data

If you modify `config/networks.yaml` or any `config/scenarios/*.yaml`, regenerate:

```bash
# Regenerate all 8 scenarios
uv run python scripts/generate_scenarios.py --all --validate --force

# Regenerate one scenario only
uv run python scripts/generate_scenarios.py --scenario SC-01 --validate --force
```

## Architecture Notes

- **Pre-computed data:** All scenario outputs are pre-computed offline and committed to `data/scenarios/`. The Streamlit app is a pure reader — no NetworkX or scipy at runtime.
- **Import boundary:** `generation/` is build-time only. Never imported by `app.py`, `viz/`, or `tabs/`.
- **Session state keys:** Always imported from `session.py` — never raw strings.
- **SHAP approximation:** Labelled "Approximate · simulation mode" in all displays.
- **AI disclaimer:** Every alert card carries "Generated by AI. Please verify with clinical officer before acting." — this is not optional copy; it is a design requirement.
